Pulse value trees: what each executive needs, and where the flow breaks

A review of the core metrics and drill-downs for the CEO, CFO, CMO, Chief Sales Officer and COO in the branched dashboard, judged as each of those executives would use it. It ends with the corrected trees as a node diagram that shows who owns what and where the roles meet. All numbers are the synthetic figures Pulse shows on data to 11 Oct.

Verdict

The home page answers "are we on track?" well, but the trees underneath do not answer "why, where and who" the way an executive asks it. Three different kinds of relationship are drawn as one ("adds up to", "explains", and "is a symptom of"), a few branches are wrong in accounting terms, and each role's four cards do not match that role's job. The fix is not more charts. It is one disciplined path from metric to driver to lever to place to action, with shared metrics owned explicitly.

PartlyIs the main dashboard making sense? The summary and cards read cleanly. The role choices behind them are off for four of five roles.
NoIs the value tree correct? Sums are mixed with context, and three branches break accounting rules.
MostlyDo the charts make sense? Individually yes. Together, each panel shows two or three decompositions of the same number that do not reconcile.
BrokenDoes the flow hold? Issues and actions are not placed on the tree, and the side panel changes the main panel underneath you.

How executives expect the information

Every executive carries the same mental path, at their own altitude. T1 is their home card. Each step down must reconcile with the step above in the same unit, and the last step is always a person and a date.

T1

Core metric

Status, gap to plan in its unit, trend. Four per role.

Adjusted operating margin 17.2%, 12 bps behind plan
T2

Components

The parts that add up to T1, shown as one bridge. Biggest bar pre-selected.

Gross margin −90 bps, overheads +94 bps, A&P −12 bps
T3

Levers

What can be pulled to move that component, each with an owner.

Cost per case +1.2%: oats +18%, only 40% hedged
T4

Where

The slice that carries the gap, ranked by $ impact: brand, market, channel, customer, site or SKU.

Sunrise Oats, all markets, $17.7M margin gap
Issue

Cause

One sentence of cause, with what it is worth for the year.

Price increase stuck at delayed retailers, $25.6M for the year
Action

Owner and date

Who acts, by when, and the state of it.

Head of Revenue Growth, finish the price increase, due 9 Oct, overdue

Lenses, not components Market share, category growth, brand health, competitor price and sell-in against sell-out explain a gap. They sit beside the tree as context and never inside a sum.

The 30-second glance

Between meetings, often on a phone

  • Four T1 statuses and the one sentence that ties the worst one to its biggest T2 driver and its $.
  • No scrolling to reach the cards.

The working session

Weekly business review, monthly close

  • Open T1, see one bridge whose bars are the T2s, click the biggest bar.
  • T3 levers and the T4 "where" table in the same unit; the breadcrumb T1 › T2 › T3 › T4 stays on screen.

The high-stakes moment

Board pack, retailer escalation

  • The issue with its path, its $ and its owner, ready to copy or export.
  • Every number reconciles with the CFO's version of it.

Where the flow goes wrong

1

Sums are mixed with explanations P1 · trust

Under Organic sales growth the block is titled "What adds up to organic sales growth". Volume 2.9% and price/mix 1.6% do add up to 4.6%. Market share and brand equity sit in the same row as if they were part of the sum. The CFO's cash cycle puts order documentation accuracy next to inventory, receivable and payable days.

Fix: every panel gets two zones. "How it adds up" holds only components that reconcile, drawn as one bridge in the T1's unit. "What explains it" holds lenses such as share, category growth, brand health and competitor price.

2

Three branches break accounting rules P1 · trust

The COO's gross margin lists distribution expenses, which sit below gross margin (the CEO tree rightly puts them under operating margin). The CEO's gross margin lists injury rate and recalls as drivers. The CFO's gross margin has no price, mix or trade, the levers that explain most of the −90 bps and the whole revenue growth story, even though the gross-to-net walk sits in the same panel.

Fix: gross margin = price realization and trade (gross-to-net) + mix + cost per case, and cost per case = materials and hedging + conversion (OEE) + inbound freight − productivity savings. Safety and quality become their own branch.

3

The four cards do not match each role's job P1 · decision

The CEO gets perfect order rate, an operations measure, but no market share card and no earnings card. The CFO has lost earnings per share against guidance. The CMO has no market share card and judges growth on shipments again. The CSO's main levers (sales against the incentive target, trade spend, key accounts, channel stock) are two or three clicks down, while days sales outstanding is a home card. The COO gets gross margin, which mostly moves on price and trade he does not control, instead of cost per case and inventory.

Fix: the proposed cards per role below, chosen from each role's responsibilities, with shared metrics marked as shared rather than copied.

4

Two or three decompositions per panel, none reconciled P1 · trust

Organic sales growth shows tiles in points (volume, price/mix) above a waterfall in dollars with a different split (volume, mix, price, trade). Gross margin shows tiles (input cost, savings, recalls), a gross-to-net walk, a gross-to-net table, a plan-to-actual bridge and a cost-per-case bridge: five views of one number and none of them match the tiles. Operating margin's tiles (gross margin, A&P, distribution, overheads) sit above a contribution margin bridge that leaves overheads out.

Fix: one bridge per T1 whose bars are the T2 nodes, in the T1's unit. Clicking a bar opens that T2. Supporting views move down to the node they belong to.

5

"Where" is not a level P1 · decision

The place a gap lives shows up as a country list in the side panel, as contributor bars, or as a table, depending on the panel. Picking a country in the side panel filters the main panel behind it.

Fix: every T3 ends in the same ranked "where" table: brand, market and channel for commercial levers; key account for sales; plant, warehouse or SKU-location for supply. Columns are $ gap, gap in the T1's unit and owner. Each row opens its issue.

6

Issues and actions float free of the tree P1 · flow

A summary row such as "Sunrise Oats in all markets, rising input costs" does not say which metric path it belongs to. Its "See analysis" link opens a panel that depends on the role: for the CSO, a cost issue opens sales growth.

Fix: every issue carries its path, for example "Adjusted operating margin › Gross margin › Cost per case › Input cost › Sunrise Oats, all markets", and clicking it opens that exact node with the action attached.

7

Two drill models in one panel P2 · friction

Tiles open a side panel; charts drill in place with breadcrumbs; "Open the full analysis" swaps the main panel while the side panel stays open. The executive loses track of where they are.

Fix: one path model. The panel shows T1 › T2 › T3 › T4 as one breadcrumb and swaps in place. The side panel is kept for definitions only.

8

Averages hide the tail, and some statuses mislead P2 · trust

Brand equity reads "in line with last year" while Bolt Brew consideration in the UK fell 4.2 points, a significant move. Category growth shows "on track, ▲" when the category outgrowing us means we lose share. Input cost inflation is judged against a 0% budget.

Fix: a T1's status takes its worst material child ("1 of 24 brand-markets off track"). Category growth becomes "share gap". Budgets include the inflation already planned.

9

Every role gets the same panel P2 · altitude

The CMO's growth panel ends with weekly sales needed for the rest of the year, a CEO and sales question. The CSO's share panel carries brand health, a marketing view.

Fix: the node stays the same; the role decides which supporting blocks show.

10

The same problem shows twice on home P2 · space

Off-track core metrics appear as summary rows and again as cards a few hundred pixels below.

Fix: summary rows are for causes found below T1 (issues with a path and an owner). Cards carry T1 status.

Role by role

Each role: the job in one line, today's home cards with a verdict, the proposed four, and how the three standard moments went. "Shared" means another role owns the number and this role sees it, never a copy with a different definition.
