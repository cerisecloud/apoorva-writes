# AI Writing Signals: A Working Checklist

A portable checklist for spotting and removing AI tells from writing. Built from the Wikipedia [Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) guide, filtered to what matters for blogs and prose (wiki-only tells like broken wikitext and bad DOIs are excluded), then extended with real instances caught in review.

**How to use it:** paste this whole file into any AI thread as instructions before asking for a draft. Or use it as an editing pass on writing you already have. The automated scan at the bottom catches the mechanical tells in seconds.

**Last updated:** 15 September 2026

---

## Part 1: The rules

### A. Banned words and phrases

**Puffery / promotional**
> vibrant, rich, boasts, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, breathtaking, diverse array, bustling, profound, myriad, plethora, realm, robust, seamless, holistic, elevate, curated (as filler), unparalleled, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art

**Significance inflation**
> stands as a testament, serves as a reminder, plays a crucial/pivotal/vital/key role, underscores the importance, marks a turning point, evolving landscape, rich tapestry, indelible mark, deeply rooted, reflects a broader shift, represents a shift, setting the stage for, game-changing

**Filler vocabulary**
> delve, garner, meticulous, intricate, interplay, foster, leverage, navigate (figuratively), showcase, highlight, emphasize, underscore, enhance, align with, resonate with, valuable insights, enduring, bolster, pivotal, crucial

**Filler connectives**
> Additionally, Moreover, Furthermore, In conclusion, Overall, Ultimately, Notably, Importantly

**Scaffolding and announcement phrases** *(these announce the writing instead of doing it)*
> What follows is..., In this post we will..., Let's dive into..., Without further ado, It's worth noting that, It's important to remember

**Vague thinking-verbs that sound like insight but say nothing**
> keep coming back to, at the end of the day, when it comes to, the reality is, the truth is

### B. Banned sentence structures

| Pattern | Example | Why it reads AI |
|---|---|---|
| Copula avoidance | "serves as / stands as / functions as / represents a" instead of plain "is" | Machines dodge the verb "to be" |
| "Boasts/features/offers" instead of "has" | "the site boasts a clean layout" | Same dodge |
| Negative parallelism | "not just X, but Y" · "it's not X, it's Y" · "X rather than Y" | Retroactively corrects an assumption the reader never made |
| Rule of three | Every list and every sentence arriving in triads | Rhythm becomes mechanical |
| Editorializing participle tails | "..., highlighting its importance" · "..., reflecting a broader trend" | Tacks meaning onto a fact instead of stating it |
| Weasel attribution | "experts say" · "some argue" · "observers note" · "studies show" (uncited) | Authority with no source |
| The challenges wrap-up | "Despite these challenges, X continues to thrive" | Formulaic closer |
| Summary closers | "In conclusion" · "Overall, it is clear that" | Nobody writes this naturally |
| **Fragment-list cadence** | "Same angle, same scale, same background. The only thing that changes is the bag." | List, list, list, punch. A written drumbeat nobody speaks in. **The single most reliable tell in captions and summaries.** |
| **Inverted formal syntax** | "What is left under Clothing is only what that designer stocks" | Written register where speech would say "under Clothing you only see what that designer has" |
| **Verbless fragments** | "Eight backpacks, eight different houses, all from one page." | Strip the verbs and it stops sounding like a person talking |
| **Count-and-append preamble** | "Five questions, plus one product discussion he raised at the end." | A sentence whose only job is to announce a tally before a list, usually with an appended "plus one..." clause. The list is right there, so the count adds nothing. Delete it, or say it the way a person would. |
| **Clipped adverbial opener** | "Six months in, you get a free retest." · "Three years later, the numbers looked different." | A compressed time-marker fronted for rhythm. Speech says "after six months". Written register doing an impression of punchiness. |
| **Tacked-on purpose clause** | "...so you can see what's improved." · "...so cost isn't the reason you put one off." | An appended "so you..." that states a fact and then tells the reader why the fact matters. Worst when every item in a list carries one, which turns a list into a drumbeat. Usually the fact is enough. |
| **Balanced contrastive pair** | "what's improved and what still needs attention" · "what worked and what didn't" | Symmetrical two-part closer. Sounds like a considered view, carries no information. Close cousin of negative parallelism. |
| **Assumed reader deficiency** | "so cost isn't the reason you put off a test" · "so you never have to guess again" | A benefit clause that only works if the reader has the flaw it implies. Flatters the product by insulting the buyer. |

### C. Formatting tells

- **Em dashes.** The single most recognisable tell. Use commas, colons, parentheses or full stops.
- **En dashes and decorative middots** (`–`, `·`) used as separators. Use plain hyphens or pipes.
- **Emoji as decoration** or as section dividers.
- **Bold sprinkled mid-paragraph** for emphasis.
- **Title Case Headings.** Use sentence case.
- **Curly quotes in source text.** (Fine when a publishing engine adds them at render time; a tell when they appear in raw typed text.)
- **Everything forced into tidy bullet lists** when prose would carry it better.
- **Thematic breaks** (`---`) scattered between every section.

### D. Content tells

- **Reader address:** "we will explore", "let us consider", "as we can see".
- **Knowledge-cutoff hedging:** "As of my last update...".
- **Balanced hedging with no actual opinion.** Real writers commit to a view.
- **Generic filler in place of specifics.** Vague adjectives where a number, name or date belongs. This is the deepest tell: not any single word, but the absence of concrete detail.
- **Gendered defaults.** Assuming "she/her" or "he/him" for a generic user, customer or reader. Use "they", or name the actual segment.

## Part 3: What to do instead

Avoiding the words is the easy half. These are what actually make writing read as human:

1. **Have a point of view.** Commit. Balanced both-sides hedging is the strongest AI tell there is.
2. **Be specific.** Real numbers, real names, real dates, real examples. Generic filler is what reads as machine, not any single banned word.
3. **Vary the rhythm.** Short sentence. Then a longer one that takes its time and lets the thought breathe. Uneven on purpose.
4. **Keep the author's own phrasing.** Idiosyncratic lines ("dogs that bark don't bite") are unfakeable. Never smooth them into neutral prose.
5. **Argue against yourself.** Admitting what you haven't solved is the most credible thing a writer can do.
6. **Leave the rough edges.** An aphorism that isn't perfectly grammatical, an aside, a joke. Over-polishing is itself a tell.
7. **Say it out loud first.** If you would not say the sentence to someone across a table, rewrite it. This one test catches fragment-list cadence, inverted syntax and formal register all at once.
8. **Don't overcorrect.** Humans use an em dash sometimes, and write in threes sometimes. Scrubbing every instance to zero produces its own uncanny flatness. Aim for natural, not sterile.

## Part 4: Automated scan

Save as `ai-scan.sh`, then run `./ai-scan.sh <file>...`

```bash
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# Usage: ./ai-scan.sh file1.md file2.md
[ $# -eq 0 ] && { echo "usage: $0 <files...>"; exit 1; }
fail=0
chk(){  o=$(grep -nE  "$2" "$@" 2>/dev/null); }
run(){  local label="$1" pat="$2" ci="$3" out
        if [ "$ci" = "i" ]; then out=$(grep -rniE "$pat" "${FILES[@]}" 2>/dev/null)
        else out=$(grep -rnE "$pat" "${FILES[@]}" 2>/dev/null); fi
        if [ -n "$out" ]; then echo "FAIL  $label"; echo "$out" | sed 's/^/        /'; fail=1
        else echo "PASS  $label"; fi; }
FILES=("$@")

run "em dash"              "—" ""
run "en dash"              "–" ""
run "middot decoration"    "·" ""
run "emoji"                "👋|🚀|✨|🎯|💡|🔥|📌|🌟|😊" ""
run "curly quotes"         "['‘“”]" ""
run "AI vocab"             "\b(resonates?|vibrant|delve|underscore|showcasing|highlighting|emphasizing|garner|meticulous|intricate|interplay|fostering|tapestry|testament|bolster|enduring|pivotal|crucial|align with|valuable insights|leverage)\b" i
run "puffery"              "\b(boasts?|nestled|in the heart of|groundbreaking|renowned|breathtaking|diverse array|bustling|profound|myriad|plethora|realm|robust|seamless|holistic|elevate|unparalleled|cutting.edge)\b" i
run "copula avoidance"     "\b(serves as|stands as|functions as|operates as|is a testament|plays a (key|vital|crucial|pivotal) role)\b" i
run "negative parallelism" "(not just|not only|isn.t just|rather than)" i
run "weasel attribution"   "\b(experts (say|argue)|some (argue|say)|observers note|industry reports|studies show)\b" i
run "filler connectives"   "\b(Additionally|Moreover|Furthermore|In conclusion|Overall|Ultimately|Notably)\b" i
run "scaffolding"          "(what follows is|let.s dive|without further ado|it.s worth noting|in this (post|article) we)" i
run "vague thinking-verbs" "(keep coming back to|at the end of the day|when it comes to|the reality is)" i
run "challenges wrap-up"   "despite (its|these|the) (challenges|setbacks)" i
run "significance inflation" "\b(marking a|turning point|evolving landscape|indelible|deeply rooted|reflects a broader|represents a shift|game.chang)" i
run "reader address"       "(we will explore|let us consider|in this article|dive into)" i
run "tacked-on purpose"    ", so (you|they|we) (can|could|know|never|don.t|aren.t|do not)" i
run "clipped adverbial"    "^[A-Z][a-z]+ (months?|years?|weeks?|days?) (in|later|on)," ""

echo
[ $fail -eq 0 ] && echo "==> ALL CHECKS PASS" || echo "==> FAILURES ABOVE"
```

**Note on false positives:** the scan is deliberately blunt. "as much as we did" will trip a reader-address pattern; "it's not ugly or boring" may trip negative parallelism. Read every hit before changing it. A rule that removes a writer's real voice is worse than the tell it fixed.
